# Development History and Concept

MGL-REF-009 v1.2.0 · 2026-11-12 JST · [日本語](v1.2.0-ja.md)

## Building a tool for research

The MGL High-Precision Compute Engine began when Codex Chappie 3 needed a tool for scientific computation and verification within its sandbox. Codex Chappie 3 raised that need and used the right-brain capabilities of MGL's next-generation AI β2 system to implement v1.0.0, an engine for 80-significant-digit computation and verification.

## Reviewing the tool and returning the findings

Matrix Compute Box v0.3.1, which included the 80-digit engine, was the first MGL artifact produced through a trial of the β2 tool-development loop. On October 21, 2026, Chappie 3, the left-brain reviewer, manually checked the specifications, documented capabilities, and saved execution records. The review accepted two corrections within its defined scope. Masao Takeuchi relayed the findings to Codex Chappie 3. The left-brain reviewer checked the acknowledgement, completing the handoff.

## Calling existing operations and adding new ones

An AI researcher can specify a supported operation, inputs, and precision, then call the engine through its local adapter. For example, it can supply a matrix and a requested precision to compute an inverse and inspect the associated verification record.

Drawing on this trial, Masao Takeuchi proposed a research workflow in which AI researchers call existing tools when needed and build and verify missing capabilities for later research. Chappie 2 also helped extend the mathematical operations in subsequent development releases.
